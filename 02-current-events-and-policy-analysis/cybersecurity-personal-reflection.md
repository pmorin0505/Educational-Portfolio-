## Essay

Cybersecurity has become one of the most important social issues of the modern world, and as a student studying the field, I have realized how deeply technology influences every part of society. The more I learn, the more I understand how vulnerable our information truly is and how easily it can be stolen, leaked, or misused.

Today, personal information is stored everywhere: health records, financial accounts, education systems, and social media platforms. When these systems fail, the impact is not just technical but social. Recent large-scale incidents such as cloud data breaches have shown how a single vulnerability can affect millions of people.

Ransomware attacks have also disrupted hospitals, businesses, and public services, causing downtime, financial loss, and erosion of trust. These events show that cybersecurity is not optional—it is essential for modern society to function safely.

However, cybersecurity also has a dual nature. The same skills used to protect systems can also be used to attack them. This makes ethics and responsibility a core part of the field. It is not just about understanding systems, but understanding how they can be exploited and how to prevent harm.

Studying computer science has been challenging, especially when learning programming languages like Python and Java. However, once concepts begin to connect, it becomes clear that all systems are interconnected. Cybersecurity functions like the “defense system” of the digital world, similar to how law enforcement protects physical communities.

My interest in this field became personal after working in the trades as an electrical apprentice. While working at an Amazon facility installing fire alarm systems, I saw a specialist working inside a secure data rack. He explained how he worked with servers and network systems that supported the entire building. That moment changed my perspective on my future.

Since then, I have committed myself to studying computer science and cybersecurity. I realized I wanted a career where I could build, protect, and contribute to something meaningful.

Cybersecurity is more than just a technical field. It is a responsibility. Data breaches, ransomware attacks, and system vulnerabilities affect real people and real communities. Understanding these risks has made me more committed to becoming part of the solution.
